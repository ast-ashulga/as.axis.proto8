    en: |-
      At dawn, the silver-footed Thetis arrives at the Achaean ships bearing the divinely crafted armour forged by Hephaestus, finding Achilles lamenting over the body of Patroclus. The armour's splendour causes the Myrmidons to recoil in awe, while Achilles himself blazes with renewed fury and joy at the sight of it. Thetis reassures him that Patroclus's body will be preserved by ambrosia and nectar against corruption, and urges him to call an assembly and renounce his wrath against Agamemnon. Achilles summons the Achaeans with a terrible cry, drawing even the wounded — including Diomedes and Odysseus — to the gathering, with Agamemnon himself arriving last, still burdened by his wound from Coon's spear. Before the assembled host, Achilles formally declares the end of his mēnis, framing the quarrel over Briseis as a catastrophic waste that benefited only the Trojans. Agamemnon responds from his seat — notably without rising to stand before the assembly — attributing his original seizure of Briseis not to personal fault but to Ate, Fate, and the Erinyes, invoking the ancient myth of how Ate deceived Zeus himself at the birth of Eurystheus, causing Zeus to exile Ate from Olympus. This mythological digression functions as a structural element of the oral tradition, in which a speaker legitimises present action by appeal to divine and heroic precedent. Agamemnon offers the promised gifts, and Odysseus — appealing to practical wisdom over Achilles' eagerness for immediate battle — argues that the army must eat before fighting. Achilles refuses food, consumed by grief for Patroclus, but Odysseus prevails, and the gifts are formally brought forth: seven tripods, twenty cauldrons, twelve horses, seven skilled women, and Briseis as the eighth. Agamemnon swears a solemn oath before Zeus, Earth, the Sun, and the Erinyes that he never lay with Briseis, then sacrifices a boar whose body is cast into the sea. Achilles briefly acknowledges that Zeus's blindness drove these events, and the assembly disperses with the reconciliation formally concluded and the Achaeans preparing to return to battle.
