    en: |-
      Book X of the Iliad, the Doloneia, opens with Agamemnon and Menelaus unable to sleep, prompting Agamemnon to tour the Achaean camp and convene the commanders in a night council. Nestor proposes that a scout be sent into the Trojan lines, and Diomedes volunteers, selecting Odysseus as his companion. Simultaneously, Hector — himself wakeful — dispatches the Trojan spy Dolon, enticing him with the promise of Achilles' celebrated horses as reward. Dolon sets out wearing a wolf-skin and ferret-cap, formulaic details that mark him within the oral tradition as a figure of nocturnal stealth. Odysseus and Diomedes intercept and capture Dolon, extracting from him intelligence about the Trojan dispositions, including the newly arrived Thracian contingent under Rhesus and his magnificent white horses. Dolon is then killed, and the two heroes raid the Thracian camp: Diomedes slays twelve Thracians and Rhesus himself, while Odysseus drives off the prized horses. Athena warns Diomedes to withdraw before further alarm is raised, and the pair return successfully to the Achaean ships, dedicating Dolon's arms as spoils to the goddess. Scholars note that the Doloneia's distinctive vocabulary and self-contained structure have led many ancient and modern critics to question whether it formed part of the original composition.
