    en: |-
      Hera, observing the devastation Ares is wreaking upon the Argives, urges Athene to intervene, and the two goddesses prepare for battle with an elaborate arming sequence that is a characteristic type-scene of the oral-formulaic tradition. Hera harnesses a divinely wrought chariot described in ornate detail, while Athene dons the aegis — adorned with Rout, Strife, Valour, and the Gorgon's head — and takes up her great spear. Hera petitions Zeus on Olympus for permission to drive Ares from the field, and Zeus grants it, directing her to loose Athene against him. Descending to Troy, Hera rallies the Argives in the voice of Stentor, while Athene finds Diomedes tending his wound and rebukes him with reference to his father Tydeus's greater courage. Diomedes explains that he has withdrawn because he recognises Ares on the battlefield and is observing Athene's earlier prohibition against fighting immortals. Athene explicitly exempts Ares from that prohibition, boards the chariot alongside Diomedes — displacing Sthenelus — and dons the cap of Hades to conceal herself from Ares. When Ares hurls his spear at Diomedes, Athene deflects it; she then drives Diomedes' spear into Ares' belly, wounding the god, who bellows with the combined voice of ten thousand warriors. Ares flees to Olympus and complains to Zeus, displaying his immortal wound and accusing Athene and Diomedes of hubris; Zeus rebukes him sharply as the most hateful of gods, yet orders Paeëon to heal him. Paeëon heals the wound with swift-acting simples, Hebe bathes and robes Ares, and the episode closes with Hera and Athene returning to Olympus having succeeded in removing Ares from the battle.
