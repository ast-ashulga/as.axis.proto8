Мерион, сломавший копьё о щит Деифоба, возвращается в шатёр за новым оружием, и между двумя критскими вождями разворачивается примечательный обмен речами о природе воинской доблести, отражающий центральную для поэмы систему ценностей — тимē и клеос. Идоменей убивает Отрионея, Асия и Алкафоя, причём последнего Посейдон сковывает на месте, лишая способности уклониться от удара. В ответ Деифоб призывает Энея, и вокруг тела Алкафоя вспыхивает схватка двух противоборствующих отрядов. Тевкр поражает Имбрия, Гектор ранит Амфимаха, а два Аянта уносят тело Имбрия, что оформлено в поэме развёрнутым симиле с двумя львами, несущими козу. Менелай вступает в единоборство с Геленом и Писандром: Гелен ранен в руку, Писандр убит ударом меча в лоб, и над его телом Менелай произносит речь, апеллирующую к справедливости Зевса-Ксения. Полидамас убеждает Гектора отступить и собрать лучших воинов; обходя боевую линию, Гектор обнаруживает тяжёлые потери среди вождей и с упрёком обращается к Александру. Книга завершается образом двух враждующих богов — Зевса и Посейдона, — натягивающих нити жестокой распри над обоими войсками, и новым натиском троянцев, которому противостоит не дрогнувший строй ахейцев во главе с Аянтом.

      The episode opens with a failed exchange of spear-casts between Meriones and Deïphobus, in which Meriones' weapon shatters on Deïphobus' shield, prompting him to withdraw and seek a replacement spear from the Achaean huts. Teucer then slays Imbrius, and the Ajaxes recover his body, while Hector wounds Amphimachus, whose corpse is subsequently retrieved by Stichius and Menestheus; in a gesture of furious grief, the lesser Ajax severs Imbrius' head and hurls it toward Hector. Poseidon, angered by the fall of his grandson Amphimachus, moves through the Achaean lines in the guise of Thoas to rouse Idomeneus, who arms himself and is met by Meriones returning to fetch a new spear. The exchange between Idomeneus and Meriones constitutes a sustained passage in which the two warriors discuss the topology of the battle-line, the nature of courage under ambush conditions, and the appropriate sector of the field to enter — a type-scene deploying the oral-formulaic conventions of heroic deliberation. The pair advance to the left flank, compared by the ancient text's simile to Ares and Rout setting out from Thrace. Idomeneus kills Othryoneus — who had sought Cassandra's hand in exchange for driving out the Achaeans — and delivers a sardonic taunt over the corpse, offering an Achaean bride in mockery. He then slays Asius, whose death is elaborated by a simile of a felled mountain tree, and Antilochus kills Asius' charioteer. Deïphobus retaliates by casting at Idomeneus but instead strikes Hypsenor fatally, boasting that Asius now has an escort to Hades. Poseidon intervenes to immobilise Alcathous — son-in-law of Anchises and thus kinsman of Aeneas — allowing Idomeneus to kill him, after which the spear fixed in Alcathous' heart causes its butt-end to quiver as the heart continues beating, a detail the ancient text presents with notable precision. Deïphobus recruits Aeneas to the battle, and Idomeneus, though acknowledging his age against Aeneas' youthful strength, stands his ground like a boar at bay and calls for allied support. In the ensuing melee, Aeneas' cast misses Idomeneus but Idomeneus kills Oenomaus, and Deïphobus' subsequent cast misses again, striking instead Ascalaphus, son of Ares, whose death Ares himself does not yet know, being restrained on Olympus by Zeus's will. Meriones wounds Deïphobus in the arm and recovers the spear, and Polites leads the injured Deïphobus from the field. Further killings follow — Aeneas slays Aphareus, Antilochus kills Thoön, and Meriones kills Adamas with a groin wound described through a simile of a tethered bull — before Helenus mortally strikes Deïpyrus with a Thracian sword and is himself wounded in the bow-hand by Menelaus. Menelaus then kills Peisander in close combat with a sword-stroke to the forehead, delivering a speech that frames Trojan aggression against his marriage and household as the underlying cause of the war. Meriones subsequently kills Harpalion, guest-friend of Paris, with an arrow, and Paris in revenge kills Euchenor of Corinth, a man who had chosen death in battle over disease at home, having been forewarned by his seer-father Polyidus. The narrator draws back to reveal that Hector has been unaware of the carnage on the left flank, pressing instead at the centre where the Boeotians, Ionians, Locrians, Phthians, and Epeians struggle to hold the ships, led by Menestheus, Meges, Medon, and Podarces. The two Ajaxes defend this sector in a sustained simile drawn from oxen ploughing in tandem, while the Locrians contribute with bows and slings rather than close-combat equipment. Polydamas counsels Hector to consolidate the Trojan leadership before pressing further, and Hector accepts, but his survey of the battlefield reveals most of the named Trojan commanders dead or wounded. Hector rebukes Paris, who defends himself by noting he has not withdrawn from fighting and that only Deïphobus and Helenus are gone, both carrying arm-wounds. The book closes with Hector leading a renewed Trojan surge compared to storm-winds sweeping the sea, met by Aias' defiant challenge and a counter-boast from Hector; an eagle omen on the Achaean right heartens the defenders as both sides clash in a din that rises to the sky.
