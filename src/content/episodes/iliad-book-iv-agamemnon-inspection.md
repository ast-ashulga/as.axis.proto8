    en: |-
      Agamemnon conducts his inspection of the Achaean forces, known in ancient scholarship as the *epipōlēsis*, passing on foot through the assembled contingents while his charioteer Eurymedon holds his horses in reserve. He addresses each contingent in turn, offering praise to those who show eagerness and sharp reproach to those who appear to hang back. Idomeneus and the Cretans receive warm commendation, with Agamemnon honouring Idomeneus above all the Danaans; the Cretan leader pledges his loyalty in return. The two Aiantes require no urging, and the simile of a storm-cloud darkening the sea conveys the mass of warriors gathering behind them. Nestor is found ordering his chariots and infantry with tactical precision, and Agamemnon laments that age has diminished his physical strength while acknowledging his enduring counsel. Odysseus and Menestheus receive a rebuke for standing idle, though Agamemnon quickly withdraws his criticism when Odysseus responds with anger. Diomedes is chided by comparison with his father Tydeus, whose legendary feats among the Cadmeians at Thebes are recounted at length as a measure of heroic excellence within the poem's agonistic framework of *timē* and *kleos*. Sthenelus replies defiantly on Diomedes' behalf, though Diomedes himself accepts the reproach in silence and calls on his companion to honour Agamemnon's authority.
