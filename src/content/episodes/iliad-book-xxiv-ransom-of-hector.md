    en: |-
      Book Twenty-Four opens with Achilles unable to sleep, driven by grief for Patroclus to roam the shoreline and drag Hector's body repeatedly around the burial mound, yet Apollo shields the corpse from corruption beneath a golden aegis. A divine assembly convenes, and Apollo rebukes the gods for allowing Achilles to dishonour a noble warrior who had duly honoured the Olympians with sacrifice. Hera resists, invoking Achilles' divine ancestry through Thetis, while Zeus mediates, acknowledging Hector's piety yet refusing to sanction outright theft of the body. Zeus dispatches Iris to summon Thetis to Olympus, where he instructs her to carry his command to Achilles: release Hector's body in exchange for ransom. Thetis descends and conveys Zeus's bidding, and Achilles, within the honour-framework of timē and divine authority, consents.
