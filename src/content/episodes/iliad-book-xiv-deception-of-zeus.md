    en: |-
      Nestor, hearing the roar of battle while still at wine, counsels Machaon to remain and rest while he himself goes to observe the fighting from a vantage point. Taking his son Thrasymedes' bronze-gleaming shield, the old king surveys the rout of the Achaeans and the broken wall, his mind divided — a simile likens his deliberation to a dark, swelling sea awaiting a decisive wind — before resolving to seek Agamemnon. He encounters the wounded kings Agamemnon, Odysseus, and Diomedes withdrawing from the fighting, and his arrival causes dismay among the Achaeans. Agamemnon, in despair, proposes hauling the ships into the sea and abandoning the campaign, a suggestion Odysseus sharply rebukes as ruinous to morale and discipline. Diomedes then intervenes, establishing his heroic lineage through Tydeus and Portheus before proposing that the wounded leaders return to the battlefield's edge to encourage the troops without fighting directly. The group obeys and marches toward the ships, where Poseidon, disguised as an aged warrior, meets Agamemnon and, after urging him with winged words that the gods have not abandoned the Achaeans, sends forth a tremendous battle-shout that restores strength and fighting spirit throughout the army.
