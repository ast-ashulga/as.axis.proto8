    en: |-
      The final segment of Book XXIV narrates the return of Hector's body to Troy and the elaborate mourning rituals that follow. Andromache, Hecuba, and Helen each deliver a formal lament — a type-scene conventional to the oral-formulaic tradition — over Hector's body once it has been laid on a corded bier within the palace. Andromache mourns Hector as guardian of the city and anticipates captivity and destruction for herself and their infant son. Hecuba contrasts the fate of Hector's corpse with that of her other sons sold into slavery, noting that the gods preserved Hector's body uncorrupted. Helen, last to lament, recalls Hector's singular gentleness toward her. Priam then organises nine days of wood-gathering; on the tenth day the funeral pyre is lit, and on the eleventh the white bones are gathered into a golden urn, wrapped in purple cloth, buried under stone, and covered by a barrow while sentinels guard against Achaean attack. The poem closes with a communal feast in Priam's hall, completing the funeral rites for "horse-taming Hector" — the final formulaic epithet sealing the epic.
