    en: |-
      Apollo breathes fresh strength into Hector, who surges forward like a well-fed stallion breaking free to gallop across a plain — an oral-formulaic simile embedded in the Greek source tradition — and the Danaans recoil before him in terror. Thoas, son of Andraemon, praised through the tradition's conventional catalogue of martial and deliberative virtues, urges the Achaean leaders to stand firm while the main body retreats to the ships. Aias, Idomeneus, Teucer, Meriones, and Meges marshal a rearguard as Hector advances with Phoebus Apollo before him, bearing the terrible aegis given by Hephaestus to Zeus. When Apollo shakes the aegis full in the Danaans' faces, their courage collapses and the rout becomes general, with individual killings — by Hector, Aeneas, Polydamas, Paris, and others — scattered across the field. Apollo then demolishes the Achaean trench and wall with the ease of a child scattering a sandcastle, opening a broad passage for the Trojan advance. Nestor prays to Zeus, who answers with thunder, yet the Trojans press over the wall and drive toward the ships, which the Achaeans defend desperately with bronze-tipped boarding pikes.
