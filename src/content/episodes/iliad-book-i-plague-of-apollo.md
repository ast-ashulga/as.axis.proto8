На десятый день «быстроногий Ахилл» — эпитет, закреплённый традицией за героем, — созывает народное собрание по наущению «белорукой Геры», пожалевшей данайцев. Калхант, сын Фестора, лучший из птицегадателей, получив клятву о защите от Ахилла, раскрывает причину: Агамемнон обесчестил жреца Хриса, не вернув его дочь.     en: |-
      Responding to the prayer of the wronged priest Chryses, Phoebus Apollo descends from Olympus in wrathful fury, his bow and quiver upon his shoulders, his approach likened in the oral-formulaic tradition to the coming of night. He first strikes the mules and dogs of the Achaean camp before turning his arrows against the men themselves, so that the funeral pyres burn ceaselessly. For nine days the plague ravages the host, until on the tenth the formulaic epithet "swift-footed Achilles" — a feature of the ancient oral tradition — convenes an assembly at the prompting of white-armed Hera. Achilles proposes consulting a diviner, and Calchas son of Thestor, whose prophetic gifts were themselves granted by Apollo, comes forward, but only after securing Achilles' sworn protection against powerful retribution. Calchas reveals that the plague stems not from a failed vow or hecatomb but from Agamemnon's dishonour of Chryses and his refusal to return the priest's daughter; restoration of the girl and a hecatomb at Chryse are declared the sole remedy.
