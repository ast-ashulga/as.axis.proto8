    en: |-
      The gods assemble on Olympus, drinking nectar from golden goblets as they survey Troy below, and Zeus provokes Hera by noting that Menelaus has won the duel and suggesting the war might be ended with Helen's return. Hera and Athena, already nursing enmity toward Troy, react with displeasure, and Hera forcefully rejects any settlement that would spare Priam's city. Zeus and Hera negotiate a bitter divine compact: she concedes that Zeus may one day destroy cities dear to her — Argos, Sparta, and Mycenae — in exchange for the destruction of Troy, and this exchange reflects the poem's oral-formulaic rendering of divine reciprocity governing cosmic order. Zeus then dispatches Athena to the battlefield with instructions to provoke the Trojans into breaking the truce first. She descends like a blazing meteor, a traditional type-scene simile in the ancient text, and her arrival fills both armies with awe and foreboding. Her mission will culminate in the manipulation of Pandaros into loosing the arrow that shatters the sworn peace.
