      The elders of the city offer Gilgamesh their blessings and counsel as he prepares to depart, urging him not to rely on his strength alone. Enkidu then addresses Gilgamesh directly, encouraging him to set aside fear and to place his trust in their partnership. The passage describes Enkidu drawing his mantle over his shoulder, after which the two heroes set out together on the road toward Humbaba. Several lines are damaged and largely illegible, but the text appears to record an encounter with an unnamed man who provides the heroes with directional guidance along their route. Scholars reconstruct the closing lines as suggesting that Enkidu, here called Gilgamesh's brother, is directed to lead the way forward.
