    en: |-
      Enkidu directly addresses the cedar forest gate as though it were a sentient being, personifying the massive wooden structure in a manner that scholars recognize as a distinctive rhetorical convention of the ancient source text. He marvels at the gate's timber, stating that he has admired it across forty leagues of travel, suggesting the extraordinary scale and prestige of the cedar wood used in its construction. The gate is described as six gar in height and two gar in breadth, dimensions that emphasize its monumental character. Enkidu further associates the gate's fabrication with the city of Nippur, a major cultic and administrative center, implying divine or royal craftsmanship. The passage is partially damaged, but Enkidu appears to express regret that he did not fully anticipate the gate's grandeur before the companions' arrival at the forest.
