    en: |-
      The river Xanthos (Scamander), enraged by Achilles' slaughter of Trojans in his waters, rises in a violent flood against the hero, hurling the corpses of the slain onto the banks and sheltering the living in his eddies. Achilles grasps an elm tree for support, but it tears free and falls across the river, and he is forced to flee across the plain, pursued by the surging current in a passage that employs the oral-formulaic epithet "swift-footed" to underscore the paradox of even the fastest of mortals outrun by a god. Overwhelmed, Achilles cries out bitterly to Zeus, lamenting that he was deceived by his mother Thetis's prophecy and that death by a river is an ignoble end unworthy of a great warrior. Poseidon and Athena appear to him in mortal form, offering reassurance that his doom does not lie in the river and urging him to press the Trojans back to Ilios. Strengthened by divine aid, Achilles advances, but Xanthos calls upon his brother Simois to combine their floods against him, threatening to bury the hero in silt so that the Achaeans will never find his bones. Hera intervenes by commanding Hephaestus to oppose the river with fire, the flames consuming the corpses on the plain, scorching the riverbank vegetation, and boiling Xanthos's waters until he submits. Xanthos capitulates, swearing to abandon his protection of the Trojans, and Hera then orders Hephaestus to cease his fire, restoring the river to its bed.
