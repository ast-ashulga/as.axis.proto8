    en: |-
      Book II opens with Zeus alone wakeful while gods and mortals sleep, devising a scheme to honour Achilles by bringing destruction upon the Achaeans. He dispatches a "baneful Dream" — a divine figure bearing his explicit instructions — to Agamemnon's tent, where the Dream assumes the form of the elder Nestor, the counsellor Agamemnon most respects. The Dream delivers a false promise: that the Olympian gods are now united and Troy may be taken immediately. The text's narrator signals the deception explicitly, noting that Agamemnon awoke convinced of imminent victory, unaware of the sufferings Zeus was still preparing for both sides. Agamemnon rises, dons his royal garments, takes up his ancestral sceptre — items marked by the oral-formulaic tradition with characteristic epithets — and makes his way to the ships. He convenes the council of elders beside Nestor's ship, rehearses the dream in direct speech, and proposes to test the army's resolve before arming for battle, a plan Nestor endorses.
