    en: |-
      Tablet VIII opens with Gilgamesh mourning Enkidu at dawn, comparing his fallen companion to a gazelle and recalling their shared exploits in the mountains and Cedar Forest. The first column is heavily damaged, though wild beasts are described as drawing near to the lamenting hero. Gilgamesh then addresses the Elders of Erech, keening before them like a wailing woman, his axe loose in his grip and his sword removed from his belt. He catalogs Enkidu's heroic deeds — pursuing the wild ass and leopard, ascending mountains, defeating the Heavenly Bull, and overthrowing Humbaba — before lamenting that Enkidu now lies silent and unresponsive. Gilgamesh veils his friend as one veils a bride and gives voice to grief with the roaring of a bereaved lioness. In the third column, the text presents Gilgamesh pledging elaborate posthumous honors for Enkidu, including a great couch, a throne among the princes of the underworld, and citywide lamentation in Erech. The episode concludes with Gilgamesh donning the lion-skin and turning toward the desert, marking his transformation from grieving king to wandering mourner.

Герой призывает к плачу леса, горы, реки Евлей и Евфрат, диких зверей, жителей Урука — всех, кто знал Энкиду при жизни. Перед лицом старейшин огороженного Урука Гильгамеш рыдает, уподобляя себя плакальщице. Он называет погибшего друга своим топором, кинжалом, щитом и праздничным убором, утраченными по воле злого демона.
