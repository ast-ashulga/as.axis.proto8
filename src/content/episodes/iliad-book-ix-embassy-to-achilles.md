    en: |-
      Book IX opens with the Achaean host gripped by panic and grief, the army's distress compared through an extended simile to seas churned by the North and West Winds — a formulaic type-scene characteristic of the ancient oral-poetic tradition. Agamemnon, weeping before the assembly, proposes abandoning the expedition and sailing home to Argos, attributing the army's suffering to Zeus's deception. Diomedes sharply rebukes him, invoking the agonistic values of honour and martial worth, and declares that he and Sthenelus will remain to fight regardless. Nestor endorses Diomedes' rebuke but redirects the assembly toward practical measures: posting sentinels, holding a night council, and feasting the elders. At the ensuing council, Nestor identifies the seizure of Briseis as the root of the army's crisis and urges Agamemnon to make amends. Agamemnon acknowledges his blindness of judgment (atē) and proposes an elaborate catalogue of gifts — tripods, gold, horses, women, the return of Briseis with an oath that he never lay with her, future plunder, a daughter in marriage, and seven cities — to be conveyed to Achilles as reconciliation. Nestor selects an embassy of Phoenix, Ajax, and Odysseus, accompanied by heralds Odius and Eurybates, to carry the offer. The segment closes with the embassy departing under Nestor's instruction, with particular emphasis placed on Odysseus's role in persuading Achilles.
