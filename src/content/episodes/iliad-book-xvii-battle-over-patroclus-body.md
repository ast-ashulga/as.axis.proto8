    en: |-
      Book XVII's central action concerns the prolonged and violent contest over the corpse of Patroclus, whom Hector has stripped of Achilles' immortal armour. Apollo, disguised as Mentes, rouses Hector against Menelaus, who reluctantly withdraws and seeks out Aias son of Telamon to help defend the body. Hector dons the divine armour of Achilles, and Zeus, observing this, foretells Hector's death while granting him temporary martial power through the infusion of Ares' strength. Glaucus sharply rebukes Hector for retreating before Aias, and Hector responds by rallying the allied forces with promises of shared glory. The battle rages across the whole day, likened formulaically in the ancient text to men stretching a hide, with neither side able to gain decisive possession of the corpse. Aias dominates the Achaean defence, slaying Hippothous as he attempts to drag Patroclus away. Meanwhile Achilles' immortal horses stand motionless and weep, prompting Zeus's compassionate reflection on the misery of mortals. Automedon, joined by Alcimedon, skirmishes near the horses, and Aeneas and Hector attempt to capture them but are repulsed.
