    en: |-
      Poseidon, roused by Sleep's report that Zeus has been deceived into slumber, exhorts the Achaeans to renew their assault, urging them to exchange inferior armour for the best available shields and helmets and to take up the longest spears. The wounded kings — including Diomedes, Odysseus, and Agamemnon — comply and move through the ranks overseeing the redistribution of war-gear, a type-scene characteristic of the oral-formulaic tradition. Poseidon himself leads the Achaean advance bearing a terrible sword likened to lightning, an image that signals his quasi-divine martial authority. The clash of the two sides is rendered through an extended simile comparing the battle-cry to crashing surf, mountain fire, and roaring wind, formulaic elaborations embedded in the Greek text. Hector casts at Aias but misses the flesh, deflected by the baldrics crossing his chest; in return, Telamonian Aias strikes Hector on the chest with a stone, sending him spinning to the ground in a simile comparing his fall to a storm-struck oak. Trojan defenders — among them Polydamas, Aeneas, Agenor, Sarpedon, and Glaucus — shield Hector's body and carry him to his chariot, which conveys him groaning to the ford of the Xanthus, where water is poured over him and he briefly recovers before relapsing. With Hector removed from the field, the Achaeans press forward vigorously, and a rapid sequence of individual killings unfolds: Oïlean Aias wounds Satnius, Polydamas kills Prothoënor, Telamonian Aias retaliates by killing Archelochus, and Acamas kills the Boeotian Promachus in revenge for his brother. Peneleos then kills Ilioneus with a horrifying thrust through the eye socket, severs his head, and holds it aloft on the spear in a gesture of martial display addressed to the Trojans, a moment of brutal aristeia that concludes the book's catalogue of violence. The segment closes with an invocation of the Muses and a catalogue crediting Telamonian Aias, Antilochus, Meriones, Teucer, Agamemnon, and especially Oïlean Aias — the swiftest pursuer in a rout — with the greatest slaughter as the Trojans are driven back.
