    en: |-
      Book XX opens with the Achaean and Trojan armies arrayed for battle near the ships, at which point Zeus commands Themis to summon all the gods to Olympus, and they assemble in the polished halls built by Hephaestus, with every river deity and nymph in attendance save Oceanus. Zeus announces that while he himself will watch from Olympus, the gods are free to descend and assist whichever side they choose, his stated concern being that Achilles, consumed by grief and rage for his fallen companion, may overwhelm fate itself and destroy Troy prematurely. The divine forces accordingly divide: Hera, Athena, Poseidon, Hermes, and Hephaestus align with the Achaeans, while Ares, Apollo, Artemis, Leto, Xanthus, and Aphrodite take the Trojan side. Before the gods fully descend, the Trojans tremble at the sight of Achilles armed and gleaming, described through the oral-formulaic tradition's epithet as "swift-footed" and likened to Ares, the bane of men. Once the Olympians enter the fighting, divine Strife rises, Athena and Ares cry out from opposing positions, and Poseidon's earth-shaking causes such cosmic upheaval that Hades himself leaps from his throne in terror lest his underworld realm be exposed. The segment closes with a formal catalogue of divine pairings — Apollo against Poseidon, Athena against Ares, Artemis against Hera, Hermes against Leto, and Xanthus against Hephaestus — establishing the theomachic structure that will govern the book's action.
