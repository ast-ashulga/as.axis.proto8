    en: |-
      Hephaestus begins by fashioning a great shield, layered fivefold and adorned with a threefold rim and a silver baldric, working with consummate divine craftsmanship. Upon its surface he depicts the cosmos itself — earth, sea, sky, the unwearied sun, the full moon, and the constellations including the Pleiades, Hyades, Orion, and the Bear, which alone never bathes in Ocean. Two human cities are then wrought: one at peace, showing wedding processions, feasting, and a legal dispute over blood-price adjudicated by elders in a sacred circle, with two talents of gold awaiting the most righteous judgment. The second city is under siege, its defenders mounting an ambush that erupts into battle along a riverbank, where personified Strife, Tumult, and deadly Fate move among the combatants. Scenes of agricultural and pastoral life follow — ploughing, reaping, a vineyard with vintagers, cattle herded by golden herdsmen, lions tearing a bull, and a sheepfold — each rendered in gold, tin, silver, and cyanus. The ekphrasis concludes with a dancing-floor compared to the one Daedalus made for Ariadne at Knossos, and the river Ocean encircling the shield's outermost rim. Hephaestus then completes the full set of armour — corselet, helmet, and greaves — and Thetis descends from Olympus like a falcon to carry it to Achilles.
