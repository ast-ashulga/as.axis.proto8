    en: |-
      Zeus awakens on the peaks of Ida beside Hera and immediately surveys the battlefield, perceiving the Trojans in rout and Poseidon actively aiding the Achaeans. He sees Hector lying wounded on the plain, gasping and vomiting blood, surrounded by his companions. Zeus rebukes Hera furiously, attributing Hector's incapacitation to her scheming, and invokes past punishments he inflicted upon her, including the episode in which she was suspended from Olympus with golden bonds and anvils at her feet. Hera swears by the Styx and by Zeus's own head that she has not directed Poseidon's intervention, attributing his actions to his own compassion for the hard-pressed Achaeans. Zeus then discloses the ordained sequence of events: Poseidon is to withdraw, Apollo is to restore Hector, the Trojans are to drive the Achaeans back to the ships, Patroclus is to be sent forth and slain by Hector, and Achilles is ultimately to kill Hector before Troy falls through Athene's counsel. This prophetic disclosure, grounded in Zeus's original promise to Thetis, frames the remainder of the poem's trajectory.
