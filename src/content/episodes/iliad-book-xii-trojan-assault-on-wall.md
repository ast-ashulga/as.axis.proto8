    en: |-
      Iliad Book XII opens with a narrative aside explaining that the Achaean wall, built without proper sacrifices to the gods, was destined to be destroyed after the war's end. The text describes how Poseidon and Apollo, working with the rivers flowing from Mount Ida, would eventually obliterate its foundations and scatter its stones into the Hellespont. Returning to the present action, Hector presses his assault on the wall with the ferocity of a lion or boar among hunters, an oral-formulaic simile embedded in the ancient Greek text. His horses, however, balk at the wide trench lined with sharpened stakes, forcing the Trojans to dismount. The prudent Polydamas advises against using chariots and recommends an infantry assault in organised formation, counsel that Hector accepts. The Trojan force is divided into five companies under leaders including Hector, Paris, Helenus, Aeneas, and Sarpedon, each marshalled to strike the Achaean wall.
