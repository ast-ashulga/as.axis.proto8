      The opening section of Tablet I contains a substantial lacuna of approximately thirty lines separating the description of Gilgamesh's nature from the plaint of the people of Uruk, and the contents of this gap remain unrecoverable from surviving cuneiform sources. Scholars acknowledge that this damaged passage likely contained material bridging the characterization of Gilgamesh as a figure of extraordinary, perhaps overwhelming power and the subsequent complaints of the city's inhabitants, though the precise narrative content cannot be reconstructed with certainty. The gap thus represents a significant interruption in the argumentative and thematic flow of Tablet I's opening columns.
