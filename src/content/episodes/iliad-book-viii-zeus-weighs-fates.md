    en: |-
      Book VIII opens at dawn as Zeus summons the assembled gods to Olympus and delivers a commanding prohibition against divine intervention in the war below. Invoking the image of a golden chain suspended from heaven, Zeus asserts his absolute supremacy over all other divine powers, a boast that functions within the oral-formulaic tradition as a formal declaration of cosmic hierarchy. Athena responds diplomatically, acknowledging Zeus's irresistible might while expressing compassion for the Achaeans, and Zeus answers her with a measure of paternal reassurance. Zeus then departs by chariot to Mount Ida, where he settles upon Gargarus and surveys both armies as they engage in fierce combat. At midday, he raises his golden scales and weighs the two fates of death — the Trojans' lot rises, the Achaeans' sinks — a psychostasia type-scene encoding the poem's theology of divinely sanctioned destiny. Zeus then hurls his thunderbolt into the Achaean host, driving them into panic and retreat.
