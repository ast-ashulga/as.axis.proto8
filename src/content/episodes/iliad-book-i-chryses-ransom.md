    en: |-
      The poem opens with the invocation of the Muse to sing the destructive wrath (mēnis) of Achilles, son of Peleus, which brought catastrophic suffering upon the Achaeans and fulfilled the plan of Zeus. The oral-formulaic tradition identifies Apollo, son of Leto and Zeus, as the immediate divine cause of strife, for the god sent a plague upon the Achaean host in retribution for Agamemnon's dishonour of his priest Chryses. Chryses had come to the Greek ships bearing abundant ransom and the sacred regalia of Apollo to recover his captive daughter, appealing to the assembled Achaeans with the formulaic address characteristic of supplication scenes. The Achaean rank and file favoured accepting the ransom and honouring the priest, yet Agamemnon refused harshly, asserting that the girl would grow old in his household in Argos, far from her homeland. Dismissed with threats, the aged priest departed in silence along the shore of the sea and offered a prayer invoking Apollo under his epithet Sminthian, calling upon the god to exact vengeance upon the Danaans with his arrows.
