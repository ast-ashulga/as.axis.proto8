    en: |-
      Achilles rallies the Achaeans with a battle cry, acknowledging that even gods such as Ares and Athena could not single-handedly master so vast a conflict, yet pledging to drive straight through the Trojan line. Hector responds by urging the Trojans forward, declaring his willingness to face Achilles despite recognising his opponent's superior might — a statement framed by the oral-formulaic tradition's agonistic values of honour and martial courage. Apollo intervenes, warning Hector not to confront Achilles directly but to seek cover within the mass of fighters, and Hector withdraws in obedience to the god's voice. Achilles then launches his aristeia, slaying in rapid succession Iphition, Demoleon, Hippodamas, and the young Polydorus, youngest and most beloved son of Priam, whose death draws Hector out of the throng in grief. The two champions exchange taunts — a type-scene conventional in oral-formulaic heroic combat — before Hector casts his spear, which Athena turns back with a breath, and Apollo then wraps Hector in mist, frustrating Achilles three times as he lunges. Achilles breaks off and resumes his slaughter, killing Dryops, Demuchus, Laogonus, Dardanus, Tros, Mulius, Echeclus, Deucalion, Rhigmus, and others, the book closing with two extended similes comparing his rampage to wildfire consuming a mountain forest and to bulls threshing grain, as blood drenches the earth and his chariot wheels.
