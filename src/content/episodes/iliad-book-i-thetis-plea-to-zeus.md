Тем временем Одиссей доставляет Хрисеиду в Хрису, совершается торжественная гекатомба, Хрис молится Аполлону об избавлении ахейцев от мора, и бог внемлет ему. По истечении двенадцати дней Фетида восходит на Олимп, обнимает колени Зевса и получает его согласие — подтверждённое священным кивком, от которого сотрясся Олимп, — почтить Ахилла, даровав победы троянцам.     en: |-
      Thetis rises from the sea "like a mist" and approaches the weeping Achilles on the shore, stroking him and inviting him to speak his grief. Achilles, addressed in the ancient text by the oral-formulaic epithet "swift-footed," recounts in full the sequence of wrongs: the sack of Thebe, the distribution of Chryseis to Agamemnon, the rejected ransom, Apollo's plague, and the seizure of Briseis. He implores Thetis to remind Zeus of the debt owed since she once summoned Briareus to forestall an Olympian conspiracy against him. Thetis, lamenting her son's fated brevity of life, promises to supplicate Zeus when he returns from a twelve-day feast among the blameless Ethiopians. The narrative then follows Odysseus to Chryse, where Chryseis is restored, a hecatomb is sacrificed with full ritual detail, and the paean appeases Apollo. Achilles remains apart in wrath, absent from assembly and battle. On the twelfth day Thetis ascends to Olympus, clasps Zeus's knees in the canonical supplication gesture, and secures his nod — described as the surest and irrevocable pledge among the immortals — to honour Achilles by granting strength to the Trojans. Hera's suspicious reproach, Zeus's sharp rebuke, and Hephaestus's comic intervention with wine restore uneasy peace among the gods, and Book I closes with the divine banquet ending in sleep.
