    en: |-
      At the boundary between night and dawn, the Achaean host gathers to construct a collective burial mound for their fallen warriors, then immediately raises a defensive wall and high rampart to protect their ships and camp. The fortification includes close-fitting gates wide enough for chariots, and the entire structure is supplemented by a deep, wide trench lined with sharpened stakes. The gods observe this construction from Olympus, and Poseidon, the Earth-Shaker, voices concern to Zeus that the Achaeans have built without offering the gods their due hecatombs, fearing that mortal fame for the wall will eclipse the renown of the wall that he and Apollo once built for Laomedon. Zeus reassures Poseidon, promising that after the Achaeans sail home, Poseidon may demolish the wall and reclaim the shore with sand. At sunset the work ends, and wine-ships from Lemnos, sent by Euneus son of Jason and Hypsipyle, arrive to supply the army. Both Greeks and Trojans feast through the night while Zeus thunders ominously, and pale fear causes men to pour libations before drinking, after which both sides sleep.
