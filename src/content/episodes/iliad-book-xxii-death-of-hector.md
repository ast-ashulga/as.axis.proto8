Последние слова Гектора содержат пророчество о близкой гибели самого Ахилла у Скейских ворот, после чего душа героя отлетает в Аид.
    en: |-
      Hector stands his ground outside Troy's walls as Achilles approaches, described in the ancient text through an oral-formulaic epithet as "peer of Enyalius," his bronze armour blazing like fire or rising sun. Seized by fear, Hector flees and the two warriors circle the city three times, passing the watchtower, the wild fig-tree, and the twin springs of Scamander, where Trojan women once washed their garments in peacetime. Zeus lifts his golden scales to weigh the fates of both men, and Hector's doom-lot sinks toward Hades, whereupon Apollo abandons him. Athena descends from Olympus, takes the likeness of Deiphobus, and deceives Hector into believing his brother stands beside him, persuading him to halt and face Achilles. The two warriors exchange formal speeches: Hector proposes a covenant to return the slain man's body, which Achilles contemptuously refuses, comparing their enmity to that between lions and men or wolves and lambs. Athena covertly returns Achilles' cast spear to him after it misses, and when Hector turns to call Deiphobus for a second spear he recognises the divine deception and understands his death is at hand. Achilles' spear strikes Hector at the throat, the one gap left uncovered by the armour stripped from Patroclus, and Hector falls in the dust. With his last breath Hector begs for the return of his body and foretells Achilles' own death at the Scaean gate, but Achilles refuses ransom in any amount and declares Hector's corpse will be devoured by dogs and birds. Hector's soul departs to Hades, and the episode closes with Achilles grimly accepting his own fate as the will of Zeus.
