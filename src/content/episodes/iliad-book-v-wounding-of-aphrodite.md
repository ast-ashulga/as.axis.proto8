    en: |-
      Diomedes, in the full force of his aristeia, strikes Aeneas on the hip-joint with a massive stone, bringing the Trojan hero to his knees. Aphrodite, identified by the oral-formulaic tradition as the daughter of Zeus and mother of Aeneas, intervenes to shield her son within the fold of her bright garment. Diomedes, discerning her as a goddess who holds no authority in martial combat — unlike Athene or Enyo — pursues and wounds her with his spear across the wrist, causing ichor, the immortal fluid that flows in the veins of the gods, to pour forth. Apollo assumes the protection of Aeneas, concealing him in a dark cloud, while Iris conveys the stricken Aphrodite from the battlefield to Olympus. There the goddess Dione receives and consoles her, recounting a catalogue of divine sufferings at mortal hands — including those of Ares, Hera, and Hades — and issues a warning against Diomedes. The episode closes with Athene mockingly suggesting to Zeus that Aphrodite injured herself while fondling an Achaean woman, and Zeus gently reaffirms that warfare belongs to Ares and Athene, not to Aphrodite.
