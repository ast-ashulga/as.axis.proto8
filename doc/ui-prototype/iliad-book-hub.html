BOOK XXIV

Inferred from partial evidence; scholarly consensus with acknowledged gaps.

Book XXIV survives in all major manuscript traditions. However, Aristarchus of Samothrace athetized (bracketed as potentially spurious) approximately 12 lines, including parts of the opening divine assembly. The overall book is secure; specific line attribution remains a matter of scholarly debate.

The Ransom

The final book of the Iliad. After Achilles kills Hector, he continues to defile the body — dragging it around Patroclus' tomb each morning. The gods, offended by this treatment, intervene. Zeus sends Iris to Priam and Thetis to Achilles. Priam loads a ransom cart and crosses enemy lines at night, guided by Hermes in disguise. He reaches Achilles and supplicates him directly. Both men weep for their dead. Achilles relents; the body is washed, anointed, and returned. A truce of eleven days is granted for the funeral rites.

EPISODES

THE GODS DECIDE

Zeus calls an assembly after Achilles continues dragging Hector's body around Patroclus' tomb. Iris is sent to Priam with Zeus's instructions: go to Achilles, bring ransom, retrieve the body. Thetis is sent to Achilles with a gentle rebuke. The divine machinery that makes the ransom possible is set in motion.

PRIAM'S NIGHT JOURNEY

Priam loads a ransom cart with treasures — gold, cauldrons, robes — and sets out alone through enemy lines with his herald Idaeus. Hermes appears disguised as a young Myrmidon soldier, escorts Priam past the Greek sentries, and guides him unseen through the camp to Achilles' hut.

◑ THE RANSOM OF HECTOR

Priam supplicates Achilles at the knees, kissing the hands that killed Hector. Both men weep simultaneously — Priam for Hector, Achilles for his father Peleus and for Patroclus. Achilles relents. The body is washed, anointed, and wrapped so Priam cannot see the wounds. A shared meal; eleven days of truce; Priam departs before dawn with Hector on the cart.
