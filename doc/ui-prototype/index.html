MESOPOTAMIAN

The Epic of Gilgamesh

c. 2100–1200 BCE · Standard Babylonian Version

The oldest surviving epic narrative. A king's search for immortality after the death of his companion. The earliest recorded flood story. The oldest question about whether a life can mean anything if it ends.

GREEK

The Iliad

c. 8th century BCE · Attributed to Homer · Dactylic hexameter

24 books. The Trojan War, the rage of Achilles, the death of Hector. The oldest surviving text of the Western literary tradition, composed in oral form before writing, committed to text in the 8th century BCE. The poem ends not with victory but with grief shared between enemies.

○ ROMAN TRADITION

Metamorphoses

Ovid

Fifteen books spanning the creation of the world to the deification of Julius Caesar. The most comprehensive survey of classical mythology in continuous verse; composed c. 8 CE at the peak of Augustan literary culture.

Translation: under consideration  ·  Expected: Phase 2

○ INDIAN TRADITION

Mahabharata

The longest poem in any language — roughly ten times the length of the Iliad and Odyssey combined. The war of the Pandavas and Kauravas, the Bhagavad Gita's discourse on duty and the soul, and an encyclopaedic account of dharma that shaped an entire civilisation's moral vocabulary.

Translation: Kisari Mohan Ganguli (1883–1896) — public domain  ·  Expected: Phase 2

○ CENTRAL ASIAN TRADITION

Manas

Kyrgyz oral epic

The Kyrgyz national epic, preserved in oral tradition by bards called manaschi. The warrior-king Manas unites the Kyrgyz people, leads them to their ancestral homeland, and dies in the prime of victory — a founding myth of collective identity. Among the longest oral epic traditions in world literature, with some versions running to half a million lines.

Expected: Phase 2

○ CENTRAL ASIAN TRADITION

Jangar

Mongolian/Kalmyk oral epic

The epic of the Kalmyk and western Mongolian peoples. Jangar, the hero-king of the paradise realm of Bumba, and his twelve legendary paladins defend the ideal kingdom against enemies from all directions. Preserved in oral tradition until the early 20th century; first written down by Russian ethnographers in the 1800s.

Expected: Phase 2

About the platform

Mnemosyne Engine is an explorable interface for the great epic traditions of the world. Every claim is marked for what kind of evidence supports it: primary source, scholarly inference, active scholarly debate, or AI-generated interpretation reviewed by a named scholar. You can read a plain-language summary or the original translation; zoom into scholarly debate or cross to another tradition's parallel passage. The depth you want is always one step away.

No claim is presented without its epistemic grounding. When a passage is actively contested among scholars, that contest is made visible — not resolved or hidden. When an interpretation was generated by AI and reviewed by a human expert, that provenance is disclosed at the top of every content block. The platform's promise is not completeness; it is honesty about what is known, what is inferred, and what remains in open debate.
