TABLET XI

Inferred from partial evidence; scholarly consensus with acknowledged gaps.

Tablet XI survives in multiple copies, the best-preserved being the Nineveh copy (CDLI P003793). The overall tablet completeness is high, but approximately 15–20 lines are damaged or missing across all known copies.

The Flood Tablet

The best-preserved tablet of the Standard Babylonian Version. Lines 1–196 contain the flood narrative; the rest records Utnapishtim's challenge and Gilgamesh's failure, followed by a coda added in the 12th century BCE by Sîn-lēqi-unninni. This is the tablet that first established the Gilgamesh epic's importance to modern scholarship when George Smith identified it in 1872.

EPISODES

● THE FLOOD EPISODE

The gods decree a flood to destroy humanity. Utnapishtim is warned by Ea and builds a great boat. The flood comes, subsides for seven days, and the gods quarrel bitterly over the outcome.

● 1 confirmed parallel

UTNAPISHTIM'S CHALLENGE

Utnapishtim offers Gilgamesh a challenge: stay awake for seven days. Gilgamesh sleeps immediately. The failure is documented — a loaf of bread baked for each day of his sleep.

THE PLANT OF IMMORTALITY

Utnapishtim's wife reveals the location of a plant that restores youth. Gilgamesh dives to the bottom of the sea to find it. He succeeds — and then loses it to a serpent while he sleeps.
